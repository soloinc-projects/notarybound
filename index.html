Check the law, not our word for it

Open any free question. Beside the answer is the law it rests on: the quoted words, the section, the handbook edition and the date the question was verified. Right and wrong answers carry a word and a symbol, never color alone.

40 California questions are free, 8 from each of the exam's five areas, each with its law.

The pass rule in the state's words

California's exam has 45 questions, 40 scored and 5 unscored pretest questions, in 60 minutes. You pass with a scaled score of 70, and a scaled score is not a percent. So Notarybound never turns a practice result into a pass or fail.

“A scaled score is NOT a percent score and cannot be calculated by dividing the number correct by the total number of questions on the examination.”

CPS HR Consulting, the state's notary exam administrator

The practice mock has the exam's shape. Results show each area, and the misses wait in Review with the law that answers them.

Checked before it goes on sale

We draw 120 questions at random and check each one against the section it cites: the answer, the quoted words and the section number. California does not go on sale with more than 2 of the 120 wrong or uncited. The check is run again whenever a question changes, and no release ships without a passing check of the questions it carries. Its date and result are on the About screen.

Drills come 10 questions at a time, weakest area first, and Today shows your exam date, the days to go and one next step.

Who it is for

People sitting the California notary public exam, including notaries who sit it again to renew their commission.

California also requires a six-hour course approved by the Secretary of State before appointment, and a three-hour refresher before reappointment (Cal. Gov. Code §8201). Notarybound is a practice drill and does not satisfy that requirement. It does not give legal advice and does not notarize documents.
